# Early learner-observation program

Status: supporting protocol implemented; **real sessions pending human participation**. No participant observations or age-suitability results are claimed. The product owner coordinates recruitment and consent. This is sampled product learning alongside lesson production, not a child-testing requirement for every lesson or a second publication gate.

## Choose a small, useful sample

Begin with Human Origins (deep time, uncertainty, inspection and explanation) and Uruk (narrative, evidence, place and systems), using their current reviewed content. Include a representative lesson whenever it teaches a substantially different reasoning demand; prioritize a new interaction, unclear age fit or observed confusion over routine repeated testing. Check historical or sensitive-content suitability before inviting participation.

Recruit through adults who can give appropriate permission; seek learners across the 11–15 range, varying reading confidence, history familiarity, accessibility needs and homeschooling arrangements. Do not treat a convenience sample of confident readers as representative of everyone. Use roughly 12–13 as the initial drafting reference, not an eligibility cutoff. Start with a manageable small round, revise the largest issues, then observe another round when there is a concrete question to resolve. No participant quota substitutes for useful evidence.

Include independent use and parent-supported use across the early rounds. They answer different questions: can the learner use the lesson alone, and can a parent help discussion without having to repair unclear UI or supply missing causal steps? Record assistance separately; never report a supported explanation as independent performance.

## Before a session (owner/facilitator)

1. Define one or two questions, such as whether the opening establishes time/place, inspection exposes useful evidence, or deliberate comparison supports revision without implying a personalized grade.
2. Select the exact lesson/content version and device. Test account/demo setup; use a separate study profile so existing progress and drafts are preserved. Use public companions for parents; do not expose a child's private profile via an adult page.
3. Obtain appropriate adult permission and willing learner assent, with a simple right to pause, skip or stop. Explain: “We are trying out the lesson, not testing you. If something is confusing, that helps us improve it.” Do not coerce participation or reward correctness.
4. Agree the delayed follow-up and what will be recorded. Recording audio/video is optional and requires separate permission; written observation is sufficient. Avoid names, birthdates, schools, diagnoses or unnecessary family details.
5. Keep permissions, contact details and raw explanations in an access-controlled store chosen by the owner. Do not commit identifiable child data, raw transcripts or account identifiers to Git or send them to general analytics/AI services. Set a deletion date with participants and retain only consented de-identified findings. Until that storage/consent process exists, do not collect session data.

## Immediate session (about 20–30 minutes, flexible)

| Step | Facilitator action | Record |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Starting context | Ask about prior familiarity and normal independent/parent-supported learning; avoid teaching the target idea. | Broad age band, familiarity and mode; no identifying details. |
| Natural reading/use | Invite the learner to use the lesson. Let them choose pace, text size, inspection and hints. Do not explain controls unless they ask or need help to continue. | Where they hesitate, misread a label, lose sequence, fail to find evidence, or need help. Distinguish observation from inferred cause. |
| Parent-supported variant | Ask the adult to help as they normally would. Avoid scripting a model answer. | Who initiated assistance, what kind, and whether parent help repaired missing content or supported useful discussion. |
| Understanding | “What was the main idea?” “Which evidence helps explain that?” “What can it not tell us?” “How does it connect to something earlier?” | Paraphrased evidence of understanding and uncertainty, misconceptions and assistance. Ask before showing an example answer. |
| Interaction | Observe write → Compare your thinking → example/explanation → optional revision; inspect evidence, close viewer, complete and reopen. | Next-action clarity, mistaken personalized-evaluation expectations, focus/reflow or touch problems, draft loss. Completion is an interaction outcome, not mastery. |
| Closing | “Where did you feel lost or need more help?” “What helped you understand?” Offer a break/end. | Specific moments and questions; preference is different from comprehension. |

Use neutral prompts (“What are you looking for?”) and tolerate silence. Do not correct during the independent observation unless continued misunderstanding would distress the learner; record when instruction begins. A think-aloud request can increase reading load, so use it sparingly and note its effect.

## Delayed recall and transfer (about 5–10 minutes, 3–7 days later)

Invite recall before reopening the lesson or card: main idea, remembered evidence and one limit. Then offer one short, unfamiliar **reviewed** source/object/passage with enough contextual information and ask what it shows, what might be inferred and what more would be needed. The editor selects and verifies that transfer item in advance; never fabricate a historical source to make a test work.

Record delay, intervening study, access to notes and adult help. Allow “I don't remember.” After the independent attempt, let the learner revisit the card/lesson and describe what helped. Delayed recall and transfer are distinct from recognition after an example. If follow-up does not happen, record `not observed`, not a negative score or a successful result. Do not change completion or access based on these tasks.

## Lightweight analysis and action

After each round, group concrete observations by explanation, media/evidence, orientation, next action and access/reflow. Distinguish actual observed behavior, participant statements, facilitator inference, adult/AI proxy hypothesis and implemented fix. Describe patterns and exceptions without claiming statistical representativeness or age certification.

Prioritize confusion that changes the historical model or prevents independent use. Assign a bounded fix and owner; use the runbook's affected-review path. Preserve unrelated editorial claims and completed learner progress. Re-observe a risky change when its question remains open; do not recruit for a small routine copy correction without a reason. Report sample limits and untested hypotheses beside findings. Public-release family/beta readiness remains a separate owner decision.
