## Map purpose

- Period and geographic extent: c. 3200–2700 BCE; the Nile Valley from the Mediterranean coast to just south of Nekhen/Hierakonpolis.
- Focus place or route: the north-flowing Nile corridor linking the southern valley to the northern delta.
- Spatial relationship the learner should understand: Upper Egypt is south/upriver; Lower Egypt is north/downriver; Nekhen, Abydos, and the Memphis region occupy different positions along one long corridor.
- Required labels or annotations: `Mediterranean Sea`, `Nile Delta`, `Memphis region`, `Abydos`, `Nekhen (Hierakonpolis)`, `Nile`, `Upper Egypt`, `Lower Egypt`.

## Confidence and uncertainty boundary

### Coordinate-verified

- The official Memphis site point.
- The scholarly Abydos representative point.
- The broad position of Nekhen/Hierakonpolis, with the gazetteer's low-confidence warning retained.

### Source-supported

- Mediterranean north of the Delta.
- A broad northern Delta and a narrow southern Nile valley.
- The Nile's northward flow and the relative order Memphis → Abydos → Nekhen when moving south.
- Upper Egypt in the south/upriver and Lower Egypt in the north/downriver.

### Approximate

- Every ancient river branch, wetland edge, shoreline, floodplain width, and the position/extent implied by `Memphis region`.
- The map's broad landform silhouettes; no line is a reconstructed c. 3100 BCE survey.

### Omitted

- Political borders, conquest arrows, routes, territorial fills, nomes, later capitals, pyramids, modern national boundaries, modern cities, the Red Sea, Sinai detail, cataracts, and settlements not required for the teaching job.

The primary reference is much broader and multi-period. The final deliberately removes that noise and uses a soft, schematic treatment. Learner-facing copy says that river channels and wetlands shifted and that the image is an evidence-based reconstruction.
