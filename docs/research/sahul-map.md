# Sahul map implementation brief

Lesson: `lesson.humans.sahul-crossing`. Module: `module.humans.sahul.landmass-map`. Media: `media.humans.sahul-landmass-map`.

## Teaching purpose and reviewed geography

Show Sunda at the Asian side, the islands and open channels of Wallacea, and Australia–New Guinea–Tasmania joined as Sahul. This is a broad lower-sea-level Ice Age comparison, not a shoreline survey for a single year in the disputed arrival window.

Primary raster reference: [Maximilian Dörrbecker (Chumwa), Blank map of Sunda and Sahul](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Blank_map_of_Sunda_and_Sahul.png), October 2007, CC BY-SA 3.0. The reference and derivative are distributed under [CC BY-SA 3.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-sa/3.0/), with credit and changes recorded. Local reference: `sahul-assets/sunda-sahul-reference.png`.

Independent geographic cross-check: [Bird et al. 2019, Figure 1](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-019-42946-9), CC BY 4.0. Local copy: `sahul-assets/bird-2019-map-crosscheck.png`. Its −75/−85 m models confirm that deep channels still divided Wallacea from Sunda and Sahul. Its hypothetical routes and launch-point arrows are not transferred to this map.

Source-supported features: relative placement of the three regions; land connections within Sahul; remaining channels through Wallacea. Approximate features: exposed shelf extent and ancient coastlines, which varied with sea level. Omitted: exact routes, first landing, boats, settlements, political borders, depth scale. No point locations are plotted, so coordinate verification is not applicable.

## Locked visual relationship

Style-only edit of the reference: retain its full extent, aspect ratio, orientation, coastline contours, island positions, and land connections. Distinguish present land, exposed shelf, and sea by color. Preserve the Tasmania connection and all sea gaps. Approved geographic labels: Sunda, Wallacea, Sahul, New Guinea, Tasmania. Labels supplement the native accessible explanation; no legend or paragraphs are baked into the image.
